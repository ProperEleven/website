Peel it back, layer by layer

The depth dial takes the body from the skin to the nerves in eight layers. Stop at any of them and turn the body to see what lies under what.

One tap shows a whole system: the skeleton, the muscles, the organs, the nerves or the blood vessels.

800+part pages
✓Origin, insertion, nerve

Tap any part to learn it

Tap a part and the rest of the body turns to an X-ray, with the part's name on a label that follows it as the body turns.

A muscle's card says where it attaches, which nerve supplies it and how well you know it. Pull the card up for the full page, in plain words. Every part in the app has a page, more than 800 in all.

Learn it, then test yourself

  • Find it and Name it

    Quiz yourself on the 3D body itself, with points, a speed bonus and a hint when you need one.

  • Today's quiz

    Each day brings ten everyday parts to find, which takes about two minutes.

  • Flashcards

    Cards come back at longer and longer gaps as you get them right.

  • A daily goal

    Set a goal for each day, and your streak grows with each day in a row you reach it.

Every fact checked against its sources

Every fact on a page is checked against published works before the page goes up, and each page lists the works it draws on, with links, so you can read them too.

  • Gray's AnatomyGray's Anatomy of the Human Body, 20th edition, 1918
  • Anatomy and PhysiologyOpenStax, Rice University, 2013
  • The US National Institutes of HealthThe National Cancer Institute, the National Library of Medicine and NIDDK
  • The NCI ThesaurusThe National Cancer Institute's definitions of the body's parts
  • Terminologia AnatomicaThe international standard for the names of the body's parts
  • The Foundational Model of AnatomyThe Structural Informatics Group, University of Washington

The 3D body is our own. We built it from BodyParts3D, © The Database Center for Life Science, and the heart, brain, kidneys, eyes and spinal cord of the HuBMAP CCF 3D Reference Object Library (Browne, Schlehlein, Herr, Quardokus, Bueckle and Börner, 2024), both licensed under CC Attribution 4.0 International.
